H2: What is Child Anxiety?
P: Anxiety is a natural emotional response that helps us recognise and respond to potential threats or challenges. However, when anxiety becomes persistent, overwhelming, or disproportionate to a situation, it can interfere with a child’s ability to learn, socialise, and enjoy everyday experiences.
P: Children may experience anxiety for many reasons, including significant life changes, school transitions, friendship difficulties, bullying, family stress, health concerns, traumatic experiences, or uncertainty about new situations. While anxiety can feel overwhelming, early support can help children develop confidence, resilience, and effective coping skills.
H2: Common Symptoms of Child Anxiety
P: Anxiety can affect children emotionally, physically, and behaviourally. Common signs may include excessive worry, nervousness, irritability, clinginess, fearfulness, difficulty concentrating, avoidance of certain situations, and ongoing reassurance-seeking. Some children may also experience physical symptoms such as headaches, stomach aches, sleep difficulties, muscle tension, rapid breathing, sweating, or feelings of panic.
P: Every child experiences anxiety differently, and symptoms may vary depending on their age, personality, and circumstances.
H2: How Anxiety Can Affect Children
P: When left unaddressed, anxiety can affect a child’s confidence, social development, academic engagement, and emotional wellbeing. Children may avoid activities they enjoy, struggle to make or maintain friendships, become increasingly dependent on parents or caregivers, or withdraw from opportunities that support healthy development.
P: Early intervention can help children build emotional resilience, strengthen self-esteem, improve coping skills, and participate more confidently in school, family, and social environments.
P: Child anxiety can present itself through a range of emotional, physical, and behavioural symptoms, including:
LI: Excessive worry or fear about everyday situations or events
LI: Restlessness, irritability, or difficulty concentrating.
LI: Sleep disturbances, such as trouble falling asleep or frequent nightmares.
LI: Physical symptoms like headaches, stomach aches, or fatigue
LI: Avoidance of certain situations or places
LI: Excessive need for reassurance or seeking constant approval.
LI: Fear of separation from parents or caregivers
LI: Difficulty with social interactions or performance anxiety
LI: Overthinking and constant anticipation of worst-case scenarios

H2: Supporting a Child with Anxiety
P: When a child experiences anxiety, the effects are often felt throughout the family. Parents and caregivers may find themselves navigating school concerns, emotional outbursts, avoidance behaviours, reassurance-seeking, or a loss of confidence in their child. It can be difficult to know when to step in, when to encourage independence, and how best to provide support.
P: At Michelle Barratt Psychology, we understand that supporting an anxious child can be both rewarding and challenging. We provide guidance and support to help families better understand anxiety, strengthen communication, build resilience, and create a supportive environment that encourages confidence and emotional growth.
H3: 1 Understanding Anxiety
P: Helping parents and caregivers recognise the signs, triggers, and impacts of anxiety in children.
H3: 2 Open Communication
P: Building supportive communication strategies that encourage children to express their fears and worries without judgement. Active listening and empathy can help children feel understood and supported.
H3: 3 Emotional Regulation Setting
P: Teaching practical techniques that help children manage worries, fears, and overwhelming emotions.
H3: 4 Building Confidence
P: Supporting children to develop resilience, independence, and confidence in challenging situations.
H3: 5 Family Support Strategies
P: Providing parents and caregivers with practical tools to create a calm, supportive, and consistent home environment.
