# understanding-adhd | Understanding ADHD in Children and Teenagers
[photo understanding]
Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition that can affect attention, activity levels, impulse control, emotional regulation, and executive functioning. Children and teenagers with ADHD may experience difficulties concentrating, managing time, organising tasks, following instructions, or regulating their emotions.
ADHD can affect different areas of life, including learning, friendships, family relationships, and everyday routines. While challenges may change as a child grows, appropriate support can help young people understand their strengths, develop practical skills, and navigate daily life with greater confidence.
At Michelle Barratt Psychology (MBP), we help families access appropriate psychological and allied health support for children and teenagers experiencing ADHD-related challenges. Care is guided by each young person's individual needs, developmental stage, and family circumstances.

# our-approach | Our Approach
## Supporting Children and Teenagers with ADHD
[photo our-approach]
ADHD affects every young person differently. Some children may struggle to maintain attention or complete schoolwork, while others may experience restlessness, impulsivity, emotional outbursts, or difficulty managing transitions and daily responsibilities.
Teenagers may experience additional challenges with time management, academic demands, independence, organisation, and relationships. These difficulties are not necessarily a reflection of motivation, intelligence, or effort. ADHD can affect the executive functioning skills needed to plan, prioritise, and follow through with everyday tasks.
With appropriate support, children and teenagers can develop strategies that build on their strengths, improve emotional wellbeing, and make everyday challenges more manageable.

# how-adhd-affects-everyday-life | Understanding ADHD
## How Can ADHD Affect Everyday Life?
ADHD involves more than difficulty paying attention. Its effects may be seen in several overlapping areas of everyday functioning.
#### Attention and Concentration
Children may become distracted easily, lose track of instructions, forget important details, or find it difficult to finish tasks that require sustained attention.
#### Organisation and Executive Functioning
Planning, prioritising, remembering responsibilities, estimating time, starting tasks, and keeping track of belongings may require additional support.
#### Activity Levels and Restlessness
Some children experience high activity levels or difficulty remaining seated when expected. For some teenagers, restlessness may be experienced more internally, such as difficulty relaxing or settling their thoughts.
#### Impulsivity and Emotional Regulation
Some young people may interrupt conversations, act before considering the consequences, struggle with waiting, or experience strong emotional reactions when frustrated or overwhelmed.
These experiences vary between individuals. Understanding how ADHD affects a particular child can help families and practitioners identify practical, individualised strategies.
# for-parents | For Parents
## Supporting Your Child Through ADHD
[photo for-parents]
Parenting a child with ADHD can bring unique challenges. Parents and caregivers may find themselves managing repeated reminders, unfinished tasks, emotional outbursts, school concerns, or conflict around routines and responsibilities.
When these difficulties occur frequently, family members may feel frustrated, exhausted, or uncertain about what to do next.
Psychological support can help parents better understand ADHD and develop consistent, practical approaches to everyday challenges. Strategies may include establishing predictable routines, giving clear instructions, recognising positive behaviour, and breaking larger tasks into manageable steps.
The goal is not to expect children to manage every difficulty independently. It is to create an environment that supports their development while gradually building their confidence and independence.
## practical-strategies | Practical Strategies for Home and School
[photo practical-strategies]
Depending on the child's needs, helpful strategies may include:
- Using visual schedules, calendars, reminders, and checklists to make routines easier to follow.
- Breaking larger tasks into smaller, achievable steps.
- Establishing consistent places for frequently misplaced belongings.
- Giving clear, brief instructions and allowing time to process them.
- Recognising effort and reinforcing positive behaviours.
- Building predictable routines around sleep, school preparation, homework, and transitions.
- Working collaboratively with teachers and other professionals where appropriate.
Strategies are most effective when they are realistic, consistent, and suited to the child's developmental stage and circumstances.
# family-support | Family Support
## Understanding ADHD Within the Family
[photo family-support]
ADHD can affect more than the individual. Family routines, communication, relationships, and the distribution of household responsibilities may also be affected.
Parents may find it challenging to maintain consistent expectations while responding to their child's emotional and behavioural needs. Siblings may also need support in understanding differences in attention, behaviour, and family expectations.
A family-focused approach recognises that these challenges are not simply a matter of poor parenting or a child's lack of effort. Understanding the underlying difficulties can help families move away from blame and towards practical solutions.
Where appropriate, family support may help parents and caregivers:
#### 1. Strengthen Parent-Child Communication
Develop clearer communication, improve mutual understanding, and respond more constructively to challenging situations.
#### 2. Manage Behaviour and Emotional Outbursts
Identify triggers, establish consistent expectations, and use supportive strategies to encourage positive behaviour.
#### 3. Build Predictable Routines
Create practical systems for homework, morning preparation, appointments, household responsibilities, and other daily activities.
#### 4. Reduce Family Conflict
Develop approaches that reduce repeated disagreements, clarify responsibilities, and support healthier boundaries.
#### 5. Support Siblings and Family Relationships
Promote understanding, cooperation, and positive relationships while recognising the needs of every family member.
#### 6. Strengthen Collaboration With Schools
Where appropriate and consented, support communication between families, practitioners, and schools to help establish consistent strategies across settings.
# supporting-parents-and-caregivers | Supporting Parents and Caregivers
## When Parents Also Experience ADHD
[photo supporting-parents]
ADHD can run in families, and some parents may recognise similar attention, organisation, or emotional regulation challenges in themselves.
Managing a child's needs while navigating your own difficulties can feel overwhelming. Challenges with planning, maintaining routines, managing time, or responding consistently under stress may affect the entire household.
When relevant, parents may benefit from seeking an assessment or support for their own concerns alongside their child's care. Improving understanding and addressing individual support needs can help families develop more sustainable routines and communication patterns.
Supporting a parent does not take attention away from the child. It can be an important part of helping the whole family function more effectively.
# treatment-options | Understanding Treatment Options
## What Support Is Available for ADHD?
ADHD support may involve a combination of approaches, depending on the child's needs, age, circumstances, and treatment goals.
#### Psychological therapy
May help children and teenagers develop emotional regulation, coping skills, problem-solving abilities, and strategies for managing everyday challenges.
#### Behavioural support and parent guidance
May help parents establish consistent routines, reinforce positive behaviours, and respond constructively to difficult situations.
#### Educational and school-based support
May include practical adjustments and strategies to support attention, organisation, classroom participation, and learning.
#### Family support
May address communication difficulties, family stress, and challenges with routines and relationships.
#### Medical assessment and medication management
A qualified medical practitioner can assess whether medication may be appropriate and discuss potential benefits, risks, and monitoring requirements.
Medication and psychological or behavioural interventions may be used together where appropriate. Treatment decisions should be individualised and discussed with suitably qualified health professionals.
